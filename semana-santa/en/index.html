From Holy Week to Pentecost

The four Gospels as one story

Download the PDF

Before you begin

Each of the four Gospels tells the story of Jesus’s last days, and each tells it a little differently. Matthew, Mark, Luke and John wrote for different readers, and each remembers things the others leave out: Zacchaeus up in the tree, Jesus washing his disciples’ feet, the criminal on the cross who asked to be remembered, breakfast on the beach after the resurrection. From Holy Week to Pentecost brings the four accounts together into one story, in the order the events most likely happened. We follow Jesus day by day through his last week in Jerusalem, to the table, the cross and the empty tomb. Then we follow what happened after the resurrection, until he returns to the Father and the Holy Spirit comes.

This doesn’t replace the Gospels. Every reading is Scripture as it is written; we have only chosen which account to read for each scene and put the scenes in order. Where the Gospels tell something differently, or where Christians understand it in different ways, a short note says so plainly.

The heart of it is simple: to walk with Jesus through these days and see more of who he is. He goes to the cross on purpose, for us. And when he rises, he comes back to his friends, including the ones who had run away. Read slowly, and if you can, read with others. John says he wrote “so that you may continue to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing in him you will have life by the power of his name” (John 20:31).

How to read it

  • Each reading is made of short scenes, each taken from one Gospel.
  • “Also in” points to the same event in the other Gospels, if you’d like to compare.
  • Notes explain where the Gospels differ, or where the order is uncertain.
  • Small labels, like Night or At Golgotha, show when and where things happen.
  • Dates for 2027 are shown with each reading, so you can read along on the days these events happened. After Easter, the readings fall on the Sundays, the Thursday and the Sunday when they took place; for In Galilee and the closing, the date is only a suggestion.
